Maximising your solar savings

The biggest factor is how much electricity you use while the sun is shining. The more power you consume directly from your solar system, the greater your savings.

To maximise daytime usage:

  • Run your hot water cylinder during the day using a timer.
  • Use heat pumps, air conditioning, underfloor heating and other high-consumption appliances during daylight hours.
  • Schedule washing machines and dishwashers to run during the day where possible.

Two main options:

Option 1: Install batteries

  • Store daytime solar energy for use at night.
  • Maximises self-consumption.

Option 2: Oversize the solar system

  • Export excess power during the day.
  • Receive credits that offset night-time usage.
  • Works best on plans with strong export rates.

Batteries can significantly increase savings by:

  • Storing excess solar energy generated during the day.
  • Supplying power during the evening and overnight.
  • Reducing the amount of electricity purchased from the grid.
  • For customers on peak/off-peak plans, batteries can also be charged during off-peak periods and used during peak-rate periods.

Example: a 6kW system installed 12 years ago is still producing large bills. Common causes and fixes:

  • Ageing inverter – inverters generally have a shorter lifespan than panels; a larger, modern inverter can lift performance.
  • Outdated panels – modern panels produce more power per square metre than older panels, so expanding the array can help.
  • No battery storage – adding batteries increases self-consumption and cuts grid purchases.

Older appliances (10+ years) can use significantly more electricity than modern, energy-efficient equivalents. Worth reviewing:

  • Refrigerators
  • Washing machines
  • Dryers
  • Heat pumps

EV charging & solar

If your vehicle is home during the day, charging from solar is highly beneficial — you use free solar energy instead of exporting it to the grid.

If you're not home during the day:

  • Off-peak EV charging plans are usually the most cost-effective option.
  • With battery storage, you can charge the batteries with solar and use that stored energy to charge the EV later.

The best approach depends on solar system size, battery capacity and vehicle charging habits.

Choosing the right power plan

Lower usage / smaller solar systems — retailers with lower import rates and simpler pricing tend to work best.

Larger solar systems — retailers offering higher export buy-back rates are more beneficial:

  • Standard buy-back: ~8c/kWh
  • Premium buy-back: up to ~17c/kWh

General rule:

  • Solar mainly supplements usage: prioritise low import rates.
  • Solar greatly exceeds daytime usage: prioritise high export rates.

Many homeowners are considering replacing gas appliances as gas prices rise, but there are trade-offs:

  • Power providers often offer lower electricity rates to customers who also have gas.
  • Removing gas may increase electricity rates and daily fixed charges.
  • Example: combined gas/electricity customers may pay ~22c/kWh, while electricity-only customers may pay closer to 32c/kWh.

While gas bills disappear, electricity pricing may increase — a full cost analysis should be done before converting.

Maintenance & cleaning

  • Roof pitch above 20°: every 1–2 years
  • Roof pitch below 10°: every 4–6 months

Not adequately — regular maintenance is still recommended.

  • Panel cleaning
  • Inverter inspection
  • Cable inspection
  • Bolt and fixing checks
  • General system health assessment

Use: clean water and soft microfibre brushes.

Avoid: chemicals, abrasive materials and high-pressure cleaning.

Dairy farms — more frequent cleaning is needed due to dust from farm roads, crop spraying and bird activity/droppings. Recommended every 6 months.

Coastal installations — require corrosion-resistant materials, stainless steel hardware and appropriate coatings on cut metal components. Recommended cleaning every 6 months to remove salt deposits.

Systems are engineered to suit local wind conditions, factoring in wind zone classification, mounting hardware strength, panel clamp locations and roof fixing spacings.

Power outages & backup

Solar-only system: No — for safety reasons, solar systems automatically shut down during grid outages.

Solar + battery + gateway: Yes — the gateway enables automatic isolation from the grid and backup power, with transfer typically fast enough that users don't notice the changeover.

No. The system automatically detects the outage, isolates from the grid and switches to battery power — no user intervention needed.

Systems can be configured to automatically start a generator, recharge batteries and shut the generator down once charging is complete — enabling largely automated off-grid operation.
